02 What is Saarthi

One record for a business
that moves things.

Road transport in India runs on phone calls, WhatsApp groups and paper registers. The load exists in one person's head, the vehicle's papers in a folder, the driver's whereabouts in a missed call. Saarthi puts all of it in one place — and lets the people on either side of a job see the same thing at the same time.

01 Nobody can see the vehicle

A customer rings to ask where their load is. The dispatcher rings the driver. Both answers are guesses, and neither is written down.

02 Work is found by phone

Loads and return legs are matched through brokers and personal contacts, so a fleet with a spare truck and a customer with a load never meet.

03 Paper stops trucks

Insurance, permits, fitness, licences, EMIs and FASTag all expire on their own schedule. The one nobody noticed is the one that halts the vehicle.

Not a truck product

Trucks, tippers, buses, taxis, vans and auto-rickshaws all run on the same platform. What a vehicle is allowed to do is decided by its declared capabilities, not by its badge — so freight and passenger businesses share one system.

Goods carrier · Tipper · Bus · SUV · Taxi · Auto — one platform
Operators

Fleet owners and managers who want one command centre instead of six ledgers.

Drivers

Help within reach, a score that explains itself, and a record that follows them.

Customers

Post a need, compare real quotes, then watch the actual vehicle arrive.

Suppliers & operators

Material yards and travel businesses selling into the same marketplace.

Key takeaway Saarthi is not a tracking app with extras bolted on. It is the operating record of a transport business — the work, the vehicles, the people, the money and the paperwork — with live location as one of the things that record knows.

Twelve named roles. Seven kinds of account.

Behind the platform

Platform admin

Reviews verification submissions, manages organizations and devices, and reads the audit trail. Nothing enters the network unchecked.

Salesperson

Sells Saarthi itself — leads, demos, referrals, tracker handover and their own commission. Sees no customer's operational data.

Know this first
One shared domain package

The rules that define the business — roles, plan entitlements, state machines, scoring, geography, validation — live in a single package used by the server and the browser. A rule cannot mean one thing in the API and another in the interface, because there is only one copy of it.

Know this second
Everything external sits behind an interface

Storage, notifications, payments, AI, maps, telematics, identity lookups and finance are each reached through a swappable provider. Changing vendor is a configuration change; no business code knows which one is in use.

Know this third
The map needs no account

Tiles, 3D buildings and terrain come from open data with no key and no quota. Only navigation and place search use a keyed service, and even that has a free tier.

Key takeaway TypeScript end to end on the web, Kotlin on the vehicle. The stack was chosen so the same person can follow a rule from the button that triggers it to the row it writes.

11 What new colleagues should know

The short version, before you open anything.

Product
It is a record, not a dashboard

Saarthi holds the operating record of a transport business — the work, the vehicles, the people, the money and the paperwork. Live location is one of the things that record knows, not the point of the product. Capacity is bought a vehicle at a time, so the record fits an owner with one van as well as an operator with ninety.

Users
Both sides of every job are on it

Fleets, suppliers and travel operators supply; customers buy; drivers do the work; associations respond to trouble; admins keep the network honest. The same job is visible to each of them, in the detail their role allows.

Core workflows
Three loops carry most of the value
  • Win work — requirement, bid, award, order, trip, delivered.
  • Run the vehicle — pair, check, drive, report, complete.
  • Stay legal and solvent — documents, expiry, EMI, toll, fuel, service.
Technology
One language, one set of rules

TypeScript across the web platform and its services, Kotlin on the vehicle, PostgreSQL as the record. Business rules live in one shared package used by server and browser alike, and every outside service sits behind a swappable interface.

Terms you will hear on day one
Requirement
What a customer needs, before anyone has agreed to supply it. Four kinds: material, freight, cab, tour.
Bid
An offer against a requirement. One requirement can attract an offer for the goods and a separate one to move them.
Order / Booking
What an awarded bid turns into — an order for goods and freight, a booking for passengers.
Trip
A named vehicle and driver actually moving against a job, with its own lifecycle and history.
Entitlement
The resolved set of capabilities an account holds — the answer to “may this organization do this?”
Tracker
Fitted hardware, or a phone acting as one. Unlocks telemetry no plan sells, because it is earned by hardware rather than by price.
Terminal
The tablet fitted in the cab. Its state is owned by the server, so a driver cannot reach a screen the vehicle disagrees with.
Association
A district body that answers roadside emergencies. Sees that a vehicle nearby needs help, and nothing more.
