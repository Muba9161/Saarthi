The actual incumbent
Verified — publicly reported, September 2026
  • Indian telematics vendors (LocoNav, Fleetx, TrackoBit, Uffizio, Axestrack) are reported to compete below roughly US$3 per vehicle per month for tracking software.
  • LocoNav pricing circulated through resellers shows a ~₹6,000 first-year device plan with a ~₹1,800 annual renewal plus GST. Dated document — re-confirm before any comparison asset uses it.
  • WheelsEye sells a wired GPS device at retail bundled with a multi-year app subscription.
  • Marketplaces publish network claims: Vahak 10 lakh+ transporters, BlackBuck ~6 lakh truck owners, TruckSuvidha 24,000+ transporters. Self-reported figures.
  • Publicly, tracking and load-matching are separate products from separate vendors.
Our hypothesis — not verified, do not publish as fact
  • That no incumbent covers freight and passenger and materials in one account at this price. Assume somebody does until we have checked.
  • That price is the decisive lever for the 1–10 vehicle operator, and breadth is the lever for 10–100.
  • That a one-time tracker charge will beat an annual renewal in a straight comparison.
  • That the real switching contest is against a diary and a phone — so our hardest job is proving software is worth opening at all, not beating a rival's feature list.

Before any comparison page goes live: get current public pricing for three named competitors in writing, screenshot-dated. Never quote a competitor's price from memory or from this document.

The offline alternatives we actually displace
  • The WhatsApp group — free, instant, and where the work really moves. We do not beat it on speed; we beat it on proof and on not losing the thread.
  • The commission broker — finds the load, knows the people, takes a cut. Beaten only where a transporter can win the same work directly.
  • The diary and the register — trusted because it is his handwriting. Beaten only when the app is faster than writing it down.
  • The phone call to the driver — the single habit we are really replacing, and the one that makes the whole case in a ten-second demo.
  • The local tracker dealer — fits the box, takes the renewal, and is who the operator actually trusts. A partner to recruit, not only a rival.
The strategic read. We are not trying to out-track the tracking companies or out-list the load boards. We are trying to be the first thing a transporter opens in the morning — which means our competition for attention is WhatsApp, and our competition for money is the tracker renewal he already resents paying.
The switch story

Current way → better way

This is the story for every ad, every sales call and every reel. Three customers, the same four beats: what today looks like, why it costs them, what changes, what improves.

10
Switch
Owner · 3 vehicles
Today

Four WhatsApp groups for loads. A broker for the rest, who takes his cut. A ₹6,000 tracker on one vehicle, renewed every year. Papers in a folder in the cab. The return leg runs empty because finding a load for it means another twenty calls.

Why it costs him

Commission on work he could have won himself, a renewal for tracking alone, and half his kilometres unpaid. His diesel burns whether the vehicle is loaded or not.

▼
With Saarthi

He sees freight requirements he qualifies for and bids directly. All three vehicles track from the drivers' phones — no device to buy. Papers sit on the vehicle record and warn him before they expire. He posts the empty return leg as a return load.

Result

₹99 + ₹75 × 2 = ₹249 a month for all three, with no hardware. One extra loaded return trip covers a year of it.

Fleet · 25 vehicles
Today

A telematics contract per vehicle that shows a dot on a map. Dispatch in a spreadsheet. Documents in a drawer. A clerk who spends his week copying trip sheets into a register, and still cannot say what last month's Nagpur run actually cost.

Why it costs him

He is paying per vehicle for tracking, then paying people to do everything around it. He quotes from last year's numbers because this year's arrive too late to use.

▼
With Saarthi

Dispatch, tracking, documents, FASTag, fuel, EMI and maintenance on one record per vehicle. Driver scores carry reasons he can act on. Cost per trip is visible while the trip runs, so the next quote is priced on this month, not last year.

Result

₹199 + ₹75 × 24 = ₹1,999 a month for the whole platform across 25 vehicles — and a clerk's week returned to the business.

Buyer · builder or factory
Today

Rings three transporters he knows. Takes whichever price sounds fair, from whoever picked up first. Pays an advance to a business he cannot check. Then rings the driver all day to find out where the consignment is, and has no proof of when it arrived.

Why it costs him

He never learns whether the price was good. A late delivery stops his site and he finds out when it has already happened. A dispute is his word against theirs.

▼
With Saarthi

He posts what he needs once. Verified suppliers and fleets bid; he compares real prices side by side and awards. He follows the delivery on a map without calling anybody, and the trip keeps its own record of when it arrived.

Result

Free, permanently. A better price because businesses competed for it, and proof of delivery he does not have to argue about.

Use the structure, not the words. Every ad, script and sales call follows the same four beats: Today → What it costs you → What changes → What you get back. Open on his day, never on our product. The product should not appear until the third beat.

The stage that decides everything is ACTIVATION. A transporter who never adds a second vehicle never renews. Measure “added a vehicle and completed a trip within 7 days” above every other number on the funnel.
Marketplace liquidity gates the top of the funnel. Awareness spend on the supply side is wasted if a requirement gets no bids. Grow buyers and sellers in the same district, in step — never nationally, and never one side alone.
