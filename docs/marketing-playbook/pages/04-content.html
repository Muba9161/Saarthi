Content strategy

Our six content pillars

Every piece we publish belongs to one of these. If it belongs to none of them, it does not get made. Suggested mix shown on each card.

12
Pillars

Educate

25%

Teach him about his own problem, using his own numbers. Never mention the product.

  • What an empty return leg costs over a year
  • The documents that expire on a commercial vehicle, and what each lapse costs
  • How to price a trip: diesel, toll, driver, EMI, maintenance
  • City no-entry timings that cost a delivery day

Post: “Your Delhi–Jaipur run: where the money goes” carousel. Reel: 20s, a hand writing four costs on a diary page. Hook: “You know the freight rate. Do you know the trip cost?”

Demonstrate

25%

The product solving one real problem, in under a minute, on a phone held in one hand.

  • Tracking a vehicle with no device fitted
  • Posting a requirement and three bids arriving
  • A document expiry warning firing before the date
  • An SOS raised and a nearby vehicle alerted
  • The driver's screen, switched to another language

Video: 45s screen recording, no music, no voiceover, just the flow. Reel: phone in the cab, thumb on the screen. Hook: “No device. Watch.”

Prove

15%

Evidence, not adjectives. Until real customers exist, this pillar proves the product, never results we have not had.

  • Pilot results from the first association, with their permission
  • Bids received on a real requirement, screenshotted
  • A side-by-side cost comparison, sourced and dated
  • Later: customer stories, in their words, on their yard

Rule: no invented testimonial, no stock-photo customer, no rounded-up number, ever. One fabricated proof point destroys the “nothing here is invented” USP permanently.

Inspire

15%

The life, not the software. This is what gets shared into the WhatsApp groups we cannot buy our way into.

  • The driver who got home because somebody nearby answered
  • One vehicle to five: an operator's own account of it
  • The women running transport offices nobody photographs
  • Night highway photography, wordless, brand only

Reel: 30s, real audio from a yard at 5am. Hook: “A saarthi is the one who drives the chariot — and knows the way.”

Trust

10%

We are asking a man to put his business in an app he has never heard of. Earn it in public.

  • How verification works, and what we check
  • What we do with his location data — and what we do not
  • What Saarthi does not do yet, published openly
  • The team, the office, the support number that a person answers

Post: “What we don't do yet” — the most trust-building page we can publish. Hook: “Ask us what it can't do.”

Industry

10%

Be useful every week, whether or not he ever buys. This is what makes the account worth following.

  • Diesel and CNG price movement on major corridors
  • Toll revisions, permit and e-way bill rule changes
  • Seasonal demand: harvest, monsoon, festival freight
  • New city entry restrictions as they are announced

Format: a fixed weekly slot, same day, same design. Predictability is the point. Hook: “This week on the highway.”

Language first, not translation later. Hindi is the primary language for Educate, Demonstrate and Inspire — write in Hindi, then adapt to English, rather than the other way round. English-first copy translated into Hindi reads like a form, and the audience can tell.
Copy bank

Fifty-two hooks, ready to use

First lines for ads, reels, emails and cold calls. Take them as written or as a pattern. Every one is backed by something the product actually does.

Pain hooks

  • “The permit expired. Nobody told you.”

Product demo hooks
  • “One registration number. Ten minutes. Your own vehicle on the map.”

Emotional hooks
  • “He broke down at 2am. Somebody was already coming.”
  • “Your driver's family should not have to wonder.”
  • “The business should run even when you are not sitting in it.”
  • “A saarthi is the one who drives the chariot — and knows the way.”

  • “Twelve hours on the road. He should not be on hold as well.”
  • “The road does not wait for the paperwork.”

Testing rule: run three hooks per campaign, same creative, same audience, same budget. Kill the two that lose after 1,000 impressions each. Keep a running sheet of winners — it becomes the brand voice.

Distribution

Where should we market?

Not everywhere, and not equally. This audience lives on WhatsApp and YouTube, decides in person, and is reached through people it already trusts.

Production

What content should we create?

A repeatable week. Fixed slots beat inspiration — the audience learns when to expect us, and the team stops deciding what to make every morning.

Shoot in one session. One afternoon in a yard with a phone produces a fortnight of vertical video. Do not wait for a crew.

Reuse rules — one idea, six assets
  • A long YouTube walkthrough cuts into 4–6 reels
  • A reel's script becomes a carousel and a WhatsApp broadcast
  • A carousel becomes a blog post for search
  • A blog post becomes the field-sales one-pager
  • Every asset is made in Hindi first, then English

Nothing is made for one channel only. If a piece cannot be reused at least three times, question whether it is worth producing.
