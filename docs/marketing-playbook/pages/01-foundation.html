Saarthi is the app an Indian transport business runs on: find the work or post it, quote it, track it live, keep the papers straight, watch the tolls and the EMI, and get everybody home. Goods vehicles, taxis, buses and the yards and buyers in between — on one subscription.

01 · Problem

Five apps, a diary and forty phone calls a day. The load board does not know the truck, the truck does not know the papers, the papers do not know the money.

02 · Solution

One account where the job runs end to end — need posted, bids received, trip tracked, documents held, cost known, delivery proved.

03 · Customer value

Fewer vendors, fewer calls, fewer surprises — and a record that settles an argument about what was delivered and when.

04 · Revenue

₹99–₹199 a month, +₹75 per extra vehicle, ₹499 once for a tracker. Recurring, per vehicle, from day one.

The proposition

What are we selling?

Not a GPS tracker. Not a load board. Saarthi is the operating layer for a vehicle business — and for the people who hire one. If a marketer can only remember one page, this is it.

Segments

Who should we target?

Six real segments, taken from the account types the product actually supports. Each one buys for a different reason, so each one gets a different first sentence.

03
Personas

Owner‑driver & small fleet

Primary

1–5 goods vehicles. Often drives one himself. Hindi or a regional language first.

Today: WhatsApp groups, a broker, a diary, a ₹6,000 tracker he renews every year.

Frustration: empty return legs, commission on every load, no idea what a trip actually earned.

Needs: work, live location, papers that do not expire on him.

Would pay for: one more loaded return trip a month pays the subscription twenty times over.

Reach Transport nagar, fuel pumps, tyre & repair shops, district associations, WhatsApp, YouTube in Hindi.

Mid‑size fleet operator

Primary

10–100 vehicles, a dispatcher or two, a manager, a munshi keeping the books.

Today: a telematics contract, spreadsheets, and staff who join the two by hand.

Frustration: paying per vehicle for tracking alone, then paying people to do everything around it.

Needs: dispatch, driver accountability, document compliance, cost per kilometre.

Would pay for: replacing three vendors and a clerk's week with one bill.

Reach Direct/field sales, referrals, LinkedIn, industry associations, trade press, Google search on tracker pricing.

Taxi, travel & tour operator

Growth

Cars, SUVs and buses. Sells journeys, not tonnes. Usually ignored by freight software.

Today: a shop window on a travel portal and a phone that has to ring.

Frustration: no way to see who nearby wants a car on Tuesday; aggregators take the customer.

Needs: demand, vehicle and driver management, and to keep the customer relationship.

Would pay for: incoming cab and tour requests they can quote directly.

Reach Instagram, tourism & hotel partnerships, taxi unions, local Facebook groups, Google search.

Material supplier / yard

Growth

Sand, aggregate, cement, bricks. Owns stock, not vehicles.

Today: phone orders, a stock register, and delivery arranged separately every time.

Frustration: orders lost to whoever answered first; stock and reservations tracked on paper.

Needs: incoming demand, yard stock, and a carrier attached to the order.

Would pay for: requirements arriving without a salesperson chasing them.

Reach Builder & contractor networks, hardware markets, field sales, WhatsApp Business, local search.

The buyer — builder, factory, trader

Free · Liquidity

Needs material moved, a truck booked, or a car for a site visit. Pays nothing.

Today: rings three transporters, takes the first price, then rings the driver all day.

Frustration: no comparison, no proof, no idea where the consignment is.

Needs: competing quotes, and to watch the delivery arrive without calling.

Would pay for: nothing — and that is the point. Buyers are the demand that makes a paid seat worth having.

Reach Meta and Google ads, contractor groups, supplier referral, SEO on “book a truck” intent.

District transport association

Channel

Hundreds of member vehicles. Coordinates roadside help. One relationship, many accounts.

Today: a phone tree and a WhatsApp group when a member breaks down at night.

Frustration: no way to see who is actually nearby and available to help.

Needs: an emergency queue, a member roster, and something worth the membership fee.

Would pay for: a service they can offer members — and we get distribution.

Reach Direct relationship with office bearers. Treat as distribution, not as a single sale.

Drivers are users, not buyers. They never pay and never see a price — but they decide whether the fleet keeps using Saarthi. Market to them for adoption and retention (safety, score, achievements, their own language), and to the owner for the sale.
Demand

The customer pain map

Four places it hurts. Lead with the red ones — they are the pains people already know they have and are already spending money on.

04
Pain
●  High-value pain — they pay to fix this today ●  Important pain — costs money quietly ●  Convenience pain — wins the demo, not the deal
MONEY
What it costs to run the vehicle
  • High Empty return legs — the truck is paid for either way
  • High Broker commission on work he could have won directly
  • High Tracker hardware at ₹2,500–₹6,000, then a renewal every year
  • Mid Toll and fuel discovered at month end, not during the trip
  • Mid A missed EMI, and the penalty that follows
  • Mid Software per vehicle for tracking, then staff to do the rest
Saarthi answers

Return loads, direct bidding, a ₹499 one-time tracker, live trip cost, EMI reminders — all inside one ₹99–₹199 subscription.

TIME
The day spent on the phone
  • High “Where are you?” — the same call, all day, every driver
  • High Finding work: groups, brokers, calls, repeat
  • Mid Comparing quotes across three separate phone conversations
  • Mid Hunting a document when a checkpoint asks for it
  • Low Finding a weighbridge, a mechanic or a pump on an unfamiliar route
  • Low Re-keying the same trip into a register at night
Saarthi answers

Live map, sealed bids side by side, documents on the vehicle record, nearby services, and a trip that writes its own history.

TRUST
Who am I actually dealing with
  • High Paying an advance to a transporter nobody can vouch for
  • High No proof of what was delivered, when, in what condition
  • Mid A licence or fitness certificate that quietly expired
  • Mid Load boards full of stale or invented listings
  • Mid A driver's record that does not travel with him
  • Low Buying a used vehicle on somebody's word
Saarthi answers

Verification for business, vehicle and driver; no seeded or invented listings anywhere; QR identity; an evidence pack on every resale.

SAFETY
Getting everybody home
  • High A breakdown at 2am on a highway with nobody to call
  • High An accident, and no one knowing where the vehicle is
  • Mid No idea how the vehicle is actually being driven
  • Mid Entering a city inside a no-entry window
  • Mid A breakdown that a service record would have predicted
  • Low Unfamiliar hazards, checkpoints and cameras on the route
Saarthi answers

One-tap SOS matched to nearby Saarthi vehicles and the district association, explainable driver scoring, city access rules, maintenance risk.

The marketing consequence. Money and Safety are where the wallet already opens — operators pay for trackers and pay brokers today. Lead every campaign with a money pain or a safety pain, and let convenience carry the demo. Never lead with a feature list; lead with the cost of the day they are having now.
