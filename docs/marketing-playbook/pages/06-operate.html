Operating rhythm

The team's daily, weekly and monthly checklist

Marketing at this stage is a habit, not a campaign. This is the list the team works through without being asked.

20
Playbook

Every day

Before 11am

Nothing here takes more than an hour in total. Missing a day is visible within a week.

  • Answer every lead within 15 minutes. In this trade a lead that waits an hour has already rung somebody else
  • Publish the day's scheduled piece — the calendar decides, not the mood
  • Reply to every comment and every WhatsApp message personally
  • Check yesterday's ad spend, cost per lead and which hook is winning
  • Ring two accounts that signed up and have not added a vehicle
  • Log every objection heard today, in the customer's own words

The one that matters: response time. It is the cheapest advantage we have over every incumbent, and it costs nothing.

Every week

Friday review

One hour, whole team, same slot. It ends with decisions, not discussion.

  • Produce next week's content in one session, not daily
  • Test one new hook against the current winner — same audience, same budget
  • Review the funnel: where did people stop this week?
  • Read the objection log aloud. Any objection heard three times becomes content
  • Speak to two customers. Not a survey — a conversation
  • Check one competitor's public pricing and messaging for changes
  • Try one growth idea that costs nothing

Kill rule: any hook, creative or channel that loses two weeks running is stopped, not “given more time”.

Every month

With Sir

The numbers meeting. Bring the honest version, including what did not work.

  • Cost of acquiring a customer, by channel, against the payback rule opposite
  • Conversion at every funnel stage, and which stage leaks worst
  • Retention and churn — and why people left, from actual calls
  • Revenue, average revenue per account, annual prepay take-up
  • Which channel is scalable and which is a treadmill
  • Marketplace liquidity per district — bids per requirement
  • Refresh this playbook: every claim still true, every price still current

Standing question: if we could only keep one channel next month, which one, and what evidence says so?

The unit economics rule that governs every spending decision. Average revenue per account is roughly ₹150–₹300 a month. A customer acquired for ₹600 pays that back in two to four months — only if he stays. This price point cannot be built on paid advertising alone. Referral, associations, partnerships and field sales have to carry the volume; paid media exists to warm them up. Any channel with a CAC above ₹800 gets stopped and reported the same week.

What?

One app that runs a vehicle business end to end — the work, the vehicles, the papers, the cost and the safety net.

Who?

Fleet owners, taxi and tour operators, material suppliers and associations. Buyers use it free.

Problem?

Five apps, a diary and forty calls a day. Empty return legs, broker commission, papers that expire unnoticed.

Promise?

More loaded kilometres, far fewer phone calls, and nothing that expires behind your back.

Why us?
Four markets, one front door Goods & passengers ₹99–₹199 + ₹75/vehicle ₹499 tracker, once 23 Indian languages SOS on every plan Nothing invented Every number traceable

WhatsApp · field sales · associations · YouTube in Hindi · Google search. Then Instagram, Meta ads and partnerships.

“Everything your vehicles need, on one account.”

Brand & creative direction

Saarthi already has a visual language and it is in use on the product and the site. Hold every asset to it — consistency is what makes a new brand look established.

Brand
Palette — two light sources, always
#18181B Ground
#062A66→#2360BE
#E8590F→#FF8C2E

Cold navy from behind or above; one warm saffron accent in the scene. That pairing is what makes separately produced images look like a single shoot.

Typography

Inter throughout, with Segoe UI as the system fallback. Headlines heavy and tightly tracked; body at a comfortable reading weight. Indic scripts render in Nirmala UI — check every language renders before an asset ships.

Numbers are the loudest thing on any layout. A price, a count or a percentage should be the first thing the eye lands on.

Logo & lockup
  • Full colour on light surfaces only. The navy in the wordmark disappears against near-black
  • On dark creative, set VorldX Saarthi as type filled with the navy→saffron gradient — the same treatment the site uses. Never place the full-colour mark on black
  • Clear space around the lockup of at least the height of the S
  • Never recolour, stretch, rotate, outline or add a shadow to the mark
  • “Manage · Track · Move · Together” is part of the lockup, not a headline. Do not use it as ad copy
Photography & illustration
  • Near-black, always. Blue hour, night, dawn. Nothing shot in daylight
  • Unbranded Indian-market vehicles. Flat-front cabovers, Indian sedans, SUVs, buses, auto-rickshaws — not American tractors or Euro sleepers
  • Both kinds of vehicle. Passenger vehicles appear regularly; trucks-only photography contradicts the product
  • No badges, liveries, number plates or text in any frame — recognisable brand marks are a legal problem as well as a design one
  • Wet tarmac, volumetric haze, deep blacks that keep their detail
Brand personality
StraightPractical RespectfulConfident, not loud On the driver's side

Saarthi is the experienced hand in the passenger seat who knows the road. Not a disruptor, not a start-up shouting. The audience has been sold to badly for years and can hear it coming.

Tone of voice
  • Short sentences. Plain words. Rupees, not “ROI”
  • Speak to one person, never to “fleet operators” as a category
  • Hindi first for the trade; English first for enterprise and buyers
  • Never exaggerate a number, and never round one up
  • Say what it does not do — that is the brand's whole posture
Never
  • Stock photography of foreign trucks or smiling models in hard hats
  • Invented testimonials, logos, customer counts or ratings
  • “India's first”, “the only”, or any superlative we have not verified
  • Competitor prices quoted from memory or from this document
  • A map of India in any published asset without a correct official outline
  • Screenshots containing a real person's vehicle, phone number or licence
  • Bright, daylight-lit creative — it breaks the whole visual system
