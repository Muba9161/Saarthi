Differentiation

Why us?

Eight things that are true of Saarthi and not comfortably true of the alternatives. Every one carries the product capability that backs it — if a claim ever stops being true, stop using it.

06
USP wall

01  Four markets, one front door

Why it matters

A builder needs cement, a lorry to carry it and a car for the site visit. Today that is three apps and six phone calls.

Proof

One requirement covers material supply, freight, cab hire and tour packages. Only verified businesses that qualify for that market can see and bid on it.

Instead of — the alternative today

Three separate apps, or a broker who charges for the introduction.

Hook

“Say what you need. Once.”

02  Goods and passengers, one platform

Why it matters

Freight software ignores taxi and tour operators. Travel portals ignore lorries. An operator who runs both pays two vendors.

Proof

Ten vehicle classes from truck to auto-rickshaw. Passenger operators get their own booking surface; freight-only tools are hidden from them rather than sold to them.

Instead of — the alternative today

Freight software that has no idea what a tour package is, and travel portals that cannot hold a lorry.

Hook

“Trucks, taxis, buses — one app that knows the difference.”

03  Priced like a recharge

Why it matters

A man with three vehicles does not buy ₹2,000-a-month software. He buys ₹99.

Proof

Free / ₹99 / ₹199 a month. Every extra vehicle ₹75. A ten-vehicle fleet on Business is ₹874 a month — the whole platform, not tracking alone.

Instead of — the alternative today

Per-vehicle contracts quoted annually and negotiated, with the price behind a “request a demo” form.

Hook

“Less than a phone recharge. Per vehicle.”

04  Hardware optional — and bought once

Why it matters

The industry sells you a device, then rents it back to you every year for as long as you own the vehicle.

Proof

The driver's phone reports position on every plan. The fitted Saarthi Tracker is ₹499 once, per vehicle, with no recurring charge at all.

Instead of — the alternative today

A device at ₹2,500–₹6,000, then a renewal every single year for as long as you own the vehicle.

Hook

“₹499 once. No renewal. Ever.”

05  23 Indian languages

Why it matters

The driver and the munshi are the daily users. Software in English is software nobody opens, and software nobody opens returns nothing.

Proof

English plus all 22 Eighth Schedule languages, each in its own script.

हिन्दी · বাংলা · தமிழ் · తెలుగు · ગુજરાતી · ಕನ್ನಡ · മലയാളം · ਪੰਜਾਬੀ · ଓଡ଼ିଆ · اُردُو

Instead of — the alternative today

An English dashboard the owner reads out to the driver over the phone.

Hook

“Your driver's app, in your driver's language.”

06  Safety is not an upsell

Why it matters

Everyone else puts the safety features on the expensive tier. A breakdown at 2am does not check which plan you bought.

Proof

One-tap SOS on every plan that runs a vehicle, from ₹99. It widens the search radius until a nearby Saarthi vehicle or the district association responds.

Instead of — the alternative today

Safety bundled into the most expensive tier — or a helpline that takes down your details.

Hook

“One tap. Somebody nearby already knows.”

07  Nothing here is invented

Why it matters

Load boards are full of stale and fabricated listings. Buyers have learnt to distrust every number they are shown.

Proof

No seeded demand, no fabricated listings, no invented ratings anywhere in the product. Every business, vehicle and bid was created by a real person, and businesses, vehicles and drivers are verified against documents.

Instead of — the alternative today

Boards where a listing may be weeks stale, already taken, or posted by somebody with nothing to move.

Hook

“Every load on Saarthi is somebody's actual load.”

08  Every number can be opened

Why it matters

Owners do not act on a dashboard they cannot check. A number without a reason is just somebody else's opinion.

Proof

Every driver-score change carries a stated reason. Every AI answer cites the records it used. Facts, calculations and predictions are labelled as different things.

Instead of — the alternative today

A score that moved with no reason given, and an “AI insight” with no source behind it.

Hook

“If we show you a number, we show you where it came from.”

Two rules for this page. Never say “the only” or “India's first” — we have not verified either, and one competitor screenshot destroys the campaign. And before advertising the ₹499 tracker at scale, confirm the landed unit cost with Sir: a price that cannot be honoured is worse than no price at all.

Focus

What should we market?

The product does a great deal. Marketing all of it markets none of it. This is the agreed split — and the reason each capability sits where it does.

08
Priority

Hero features

Lead with these

Roughly 70% of everything we publish. Each one is a pain the customer already pays somebody to solve.

  • Live tracking with no device to buy — removes the single biggest objection and the single biggest upfront cost
  • Post a need, get competing bids — the only feature that makes him money rather than saving it
  • ₹99 / ₹199 / ₹75 per vehicle — the price is the campaign; it reframes the whole category
  • One-tap SOS — the most shareable and most emotional thing we have
  • Documents that warn before they expire — a fine everyone in the trade has personally paid

Supporting features

Depth

They answer “is that all it does?” Use them in demos, comparison pages and second-touch content — never as a campaign headline.

  • FASTag balance, toll crossings and cost per plaza
  • Vehicle loans, EMI schedules and reminders
  • Maintenance, service history, parts and warranty
  • Explainable driver scoring and achievements
  • Fuel records and cost per kilometre
  • Nearby services and published fuel prices
  • QR identity for drivers and vehicles
  • Used-vehicle marketplace with an evidence pack
  • Trip replay and delivery proof

Differentiators

Win the comparison

Deploy these the moment a prospect names a competitor, and on every comparison page.

  • 23 Indian languages — very hard to copy, instantly obvious in a screenshot
  • Goods and passengers together — nobody has to choose a category
  • ₹499 tracker, bought once — directly attacks the rental model the category runs on
  • No fabricated listings or ratings — the trust claim a load board cannot make
  • Explainable numbers and cited AI — separates us from “AI-powered” claims
  • Association network — a distribution asset as much as a feature
  • Return loads and city access rules — specific, and specific sells

Do not lead with

Hold back

Real, useful, and wrong to headline — each would either overpromise or talk past the buyer.

  • AI Copilot & daily brief — genuinely useful, but the brief is rule-based today. Show it; do not build the brand on it
  • 3D maps, terrain and chase camera — demos beautifully, reads as a toy to a man counting diesel
  • Predictive maintenance — rule-based, not learned. “Predictive” invites a claim we cannot defend
  • API access and SSO — matters to a handful of enterprise buyers; invisible to everyone else
  • Telemetry intelligence & multi-camera — needs fitted hardware, so it contradicts our own “no device needed” hook
  • Anything about the technology stack — the customer is buying an outcome, not an architecture
How to use this board. Every campaign leads with one hero feature, proves it with one differentiator, and keeps the supporting list for the landing page. If a piece of content opens with something from the fourth column, it goes back for a rewrite.
