The sales message

Three lengths, and the eight objections that will come back. Learn the answers — and notice that several of them are an admission. Admitting what we do not have yet is what makes the rest believable.

Pitch

“You post what you need, or you bid on what somebody else posted. The moment you accept, the trip exists and tracks itself — no device to buy, because the driver's phone already reports where it is. Papers, FASTag, toll, EMI and service history sit on the same vehicle record, and the papers warn you before they expire. ₹99 a month for your own vehicles, ₹199 for everything, ₹75 for each extra vehicle. 60 seconds — problem, solution, value, ask

Problem. “Most transporters run the business across five apps, a diary and forty phone calls a day. The load board does not know your truck, your truck does not know your papers, and your papers do not know your money. So you do all the joining, personally, every day.”

Solution. “Saarthi puts all of it in one account. Say what you need once and verified businesses bid on it. Accept one and the trip starts tracking itself. Every vehicle carries its own papers, tolls, EMI, fuel and service history, and tells you before something expires. If a driver is in trouble, one tap alerts the vehicles nearest to him.”

Value. “Three vehicles cost you ₹249 a month with no hardware at all. One extra loaded return trip pays for the year. And your drivers use it in their own language, which is the difference between software you bought and software that gets opened.”

Ask. “Give me one registration number. I will set the vehicle up now, you will see it on the map before I leave, and it costs you nothing to keep using it.”

ObjectionResponse
“I already have a GPS tracker.” Keep it. Saarthi does not need one — and it does the twelve things your tracker does not: bidding, documents, toll, EMI, maintenance, driver scoring. Then compare your renewal against ₹99 a month.
“My driver will never use an app.” He mostly does not have to — position comes from the phone in his pocket. When he does open it, it is in his language. Show him the driver screen in Hindi and let him decide.
“₹99 is too cheap. What is the catch?” Our mapping and routing run on open data with no per-request licence, so our cost per vehicle is genuinely low. We would rather have ten thousand transporters at ₹99 than fifty at ₹5,000. There is no catch and no lock-in — it is monthly.
“Who else is using it?” Answer honestly: “We are starting with district associations, and you would be early. That is why you get our phone number, not a ticket queue.” Never invent a customer name or a number.
“Will I actually get loads on it?” Answer honestly: a marketplace is worth exactly what is on it, and we are building it district by district. Buy it to run your vehicles. The work that comes through it is upside, and we will tell you straight when your district is live.
“I do not trust putting my business in an app.” Then do not. Put one vehicle in it, free, and carry on exactly as you do today. If in a month it has not saved you a single phone call, stop.
“What if you shut down?” Fair question. There is no contract, no hardware you are stuck with and no annual payment we are holding. You are never more than one month committed, and your records stay yours.
“I will think about it.” “Think about it with the app open. One number plate, ten minutes, and it costs nothing.” Setting up the first vehicle is the close — never leave without it.
The rule that holds this together. Two of these answers are admissions — we have no customers yet, and the marketplace is thin. Say both out loud, early, unprompted. A salesperson who volunteers the weakness is believed on the strengths; one who is caught hiding it loses the deal and the district.

The number that reframes the category
₹75
per vehicle, per month, everything included

Roughly US$0.90. Indian telematics vendors are reported to compete below US$3 per vehicle per month for tracking alone. Lead with this number for any fleet above five vehicles.

Why the pricing is shaped this way — explain it, it builds trust
  • Every plan starts at one vehicle. A bundle of five overcharges the man with two.
  • Extra vehicles cost the same flat amount each, so nobody is told their next vehicle needs a bigger plan.
  • The tracker is charged once because it is a device, not a service. Renting a box already bolted to somebody's vehicle is how dashboards go dark.
  • An operator running more vehicles than their plan covers is never cut off.
  • Annual is ten months for twelve on every plan and on the vehicle add-on — the same discount everywhere, so nobody has to work it out.
  • SOS is on every plan that runs a vehicle, including the cheapest. Safety was never going to be the thing we upsell.
  • Free covers no vehicles because a free account does not operate one. It is a description of that customer, not a limit to push him past.
Built, but not yet sellable — know this before you promise anything
  • There is no self-serve checkout. Plans, limits and entitlements are enforced, but a customer cannot pay online today. Every early sale is collected manually.
  • No payment or settlement layer. Saarthi does not touch the money between a buyer and a transporter yet.
  • Commission rates for the referral programme are not set. The mechanism exists; the numbers do not. Do not quote a rate.

Consequence for marketing: the first campaigns sell a start, not a purchase. Optimise for accounts created and vehicles added — the money is collected in a conversation.

Pricing hypothesis — internal only, never customer-facing
  • Transaction revenue. A staged customer payment (an advance on confirmation, the balance on delivery) is specified but not built. If it ships, a small platform fee on settled value could exceed subscription revenue at scale.
  • Marketplace take rate. Untested. Charging on a marketplace this thin would kill the liquidity we do not yet have. Revisit only once requirements reliably attract bids.
  • Association licence. A per-member annual fee paid by the association rather than the member. Worth testing — it converts a hundred sales into one.
  • Tracker margin. ₹499 one-time must be confirmed against landed cost before it is advertised at scale.
  • Enterprise tier. API access and SSO already exist on Business. A negotiated contract for 100+ vehicle fleets and shippers is the obvious high-value line — and the one furthest from our current audience.
  • Used-vehicle listings. A fee to publish a vehicle for sale with its verified evidence pack. Low volume, high intent, and it costs us nothing to offer.
  • Partner commissions. Fuel, tyre, insurance and finance referrals sit naturally beside records we already hold. Attractive, and the fastest way to lose trust if it ever changes what the product recommends.

No projection in this document promises profitability. These are lines to test, and each one needs Sir's sign-off before it reaches a customer.

Execution

The first 30 days

A pre-launch plan, because that is where we are. The goal of month one is not revenue — it is a district where the marketplace visibly works, and a message we have tested on real people.

19
30 days

Week 1

Positioning

Decide what we say, and to whom. Nothing is published this week.

  • Pick one district and commit to it. Everything else waits
  • Interview 10 transporters and 5 buyers in that district. Record the words they use
  • Verify three competitors' current public pricing, screenshot-dated
  • Confirm the ₹499 tracker margin and the referral commission rates with Sir
  • Lock the message on page 7 against what the interviews actually said
  • Brief the field team on the eight objections and the two admissions

Done when: we can state the message in one sentence and ten transporters have already heard it.

Week 2

Assets

Build only what week 3 will actually use. Nothing speculative.

  • Landing page with the price visible and no form in front of it
  • Waitlist or sign-up destination, and WhatsApp Business set up properly
  • One afternoon in a yard: shoot a fortnight of vertical video in one session
  • 6 reels and 2 carousels, Hindi first
  • Field one-pager, the 10-minute demo script, printed referral QR
  • Association briefing pack for experiment 01

Done when: a salesperson can walk into a yard and close without asking marketing for anything.

Week 3

Contact

Everything meets a real customer. Supply and demand go live together, in the same district.

  • Launch experiment 01 (association) and 02 (buyers) together
  • Start experiment 03 — ₹15,000 of Meta ads, 3 hooks, 2 districts
  • Begin publishing daily against the fixed weekly calendar
  • Field sales in the transport nagar every working day
  • Open the tracker pre-order intent page
  • Onboard every account personally — by hand, by phone, no exceptions

Done when: real requirements are being posted and real businesses are bidding on them.

Week 4

Read & double down

Decide what to stop. Stopping is the point of this week.

  • Kill the two losing ad hooks; put the whole budget behind the winner
  • Measure the only number that matters: bids per requirement within 6 hours
  • Measure activation — vehicle added and a trip completed within 7 days
  • Ring every account that signed up and did nothing. Ask why, and write it down
  • Offer annual prepay to everyone past day 30 (experiment 06)
  • Write the month-one honest review: what worked, what we were wrong about

Done when: we know which single channel to put month two's money into — and which to stop.
